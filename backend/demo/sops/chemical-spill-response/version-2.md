# Chemical Spill Response (Demo)

## 1. Purpose

This procedure describes how to respond to a chemical spill in the lab.

## 2. Scope

Spills of liquid or solid chemicals inside the lab rooms.

**Disclaimer: This is a demonstrational document only and should not be used for actual laboratory work without verification by qualified personnel.**

## 3. Responsibilities

* Everyone working in the lab must know where the spill kit is.
* The lab manager restocks the spill kit after each use and reviews every incident report.

## 4. Spill Kit Contents

1. Absorbent pads and granules
2. Neutralizers for acids and bases
3. Nitrile gloves and safety goggles
4. Dustpan and scoop
5. Heavy-duty waste bags and labels

## 5. Decide the Spill Size

| Spill | Examples | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Minor** | Less than 100 mL of a low-hazard chemical | Clean up yourself using section 6. |
| **Major** | More than 100 mL, toxic or volatile chemicals, or any spill you are unsure about | Leave the room, close the door, and call the emergency number. Do not clean up. |

If someone is contaminated, use the safety shower or eyewash for at least 15 minutes before anything else.

## 6. Minor Spill Procedure

1. **Alert:** Tell people nearby about the spill.
2. **Check:** Read the safety data sheet for the chemical.
3. **Protect:** Put on gloves and goggles before you approach the spill.
4. **Contain:** Surround the spill with absorbent to stop it spreading.
5. **Neutralize:** For acids or bases, apply the matching neutralizer first.
6. **Absorb:** Cover the spill with absorbent and wait until the liquid is taken up.
7. **Collect:** Scoop the material into a waste bag and seal it.
8. **Clean:** Wipe the area with water and detergent.
9. **Dispose:** Label the bag with the chemical name and hand it in as chemical waste.

## 7. After the Spill

1. Fill in an incident report the same day, including near misses.
2. Tell the lab manager so the spill kit can be restocked.
